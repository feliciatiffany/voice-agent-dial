User: why the sky is blue?
Claude: The sky appears blue due to a phenomenon called **Rayleigh scattering**.

Here's how it works:

## The Simple Explanation
When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it collides with tiny gas molecules (mainly nitrogen and oxygen). These molecules scatter the light in all directions, but they scatter shorter wavelengths (blue and violet) much more than longer wavelengths (red, orange, yellow).

## Why Blue and Not Violet?
You might wonder: if violet has an even shorter wavelength than blue, why isn't the sky violet? Three reasons:
1. The sun emits more blue light than violet
2. Our eyes are more sensitive to blue than violet
3. Some violet light is absorbed by the upper atmosphere

## Different Times, Different Colors
This same scattering effect explains why:
- **Sunsets are red/orange**: When the sun is low, light travels through more atmosphere. Most blue light gets scattered away before reaching us, leaving the longer wavelengths (reds/oranges)
- **The sky is darker at higher altitudes**: Less atmosphere above means less scattering

Without our atmosphere, the sky would appear black (like in space), and the sun would look white!
